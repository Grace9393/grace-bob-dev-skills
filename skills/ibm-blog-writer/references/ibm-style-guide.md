# IBM Style Guide Quick Reference

This is a condensed reference for blog writing. For comprehensive details, see `ibm-style-documentation.md`.

## Tone and Voice

**Primary goal**: Convey information in a direct, clear, and thoughtful way.

**Tone varies by content type**:
- Marketing/blog content: Friendly, relatively informal, engaging
- Technical documentation: Direct, concise, task-focused

**Guidelines for all tones**:
- Write clearly and directly
- Use second person (you) where possible
- Use first person (we, our) for IBM where appropriate
- Write content that is universal and timeless
- Consider diverse global audiences

## Conversational Style

Conversational style is simple, clear, easy to understand, and approachable.

**Tips for conversational writing**:
- Keep sentences to 32 words or fewer on average
- Vary sentence lengths naturally
- Use simple verbs and tenses
- Use active voice and present tense
- Avoid slang, jargon, humour, sarcasm, idioms, and metaphors

**Avoid common mistakes**:
- Making everything a question or exclamation
- Overusing sentence fragments
- Using overly casual language that undermines credibility

## Headings and Titles

**Capitalisation**:
- Use sentence-style capitalisation for headings, titles, banners
- Use headline-style capitalisation for book titles, video titles, event titles

**Structure**:
- Don't place one heading directly after another
- Ensure each heading is followed by text
- Don't end headings with periods or colons
- Use colon to separate heading from subheading (no space before, one space after)

## Inclusive Language

**Cultural and racial bias**:
- Don't use "white" or "black" where white = good or black = bad (e.g., avoid "whitelist", "blacklist")
- Don't pair "master" and "slave" in IT contexts
- Use alternatives: "allowlist/blocklist", "primary/secondary", "main/replica"

**Gender bias**:
- Avoid gender-specific pronouns unless referring to a specific named person
- Use "they/their" for singular indefinite references
- Rewrite sentences to avoid gendered language where possible

## Abbreviations and Acronyms

- Spell out on first use, followed by abbreviation in parentheses
- Don't abbreviate IBM product names unless branded that way (e.g., IBM Db2)
- Don't use Latin abbreviations: use "for example" not "e.g.", "that is" not "i.e.", rewrite rather than use "etc."

## Capitalisation

- Use sentence-style capitalisation for titles, headlines, subheads
- Capitalise only proper nouns and IBM product names

## Citations

- Provide citations for quotes, claims, or copyrighted material
- Place citation number outside punctuation
- Format: "1. Title of document, publisher, date of publication"
- Use international date format: day month year

## Commas

- Don't use Oxford/serial comma unless needed for clarity
- Keep serial commas in legal disclaimers

## Contractions

- Use contractions for conversational tone

## Currency

- Use 3-letter ISO 4217 codes (USD, GBP) instead of symbols for international audiences
- Place one space between code and amount
- Don't use K for thousand or M for million unless space-constrained

## Dashes

- En dash (–): ranges (e.g., 2020–2024)
- Em dash (—): pauses or for effect
- No spaces before or after dashes

## Dates

- International format: day month year (e.g., 19 December 2022)
- Month and year only: no comma (e.g., December 2022)
- Don't use all-numeric format (12/19/2022)

## Ellipses

- Use for omissions, pauses, or effect
- Avoid at beginning or end of quotes
- One space before and after; no spaces within

## Hyphenation

- Hyphenate compound adjectives modifying a noun
- Don't hyphenate prefixes in general

## Lists

- Complete sentence lead-in: end with period
- Fragment lead-in: end with colon
- Use periods for complete sentence bullets
- Maintain parallel construction and similar length

## Numbers

- Use numerals instead of words in general
- Don't start sentences with numerals unless space-constrained

## Parallelism

- Use parallel construction for similar elements (lists, cards, features)
- Start all list items with same part of speech

## Periods

- Don't use in titles, subtitles, headlines unless two complete sentences
- Don't use with abbreviations (US, GDPR)

## Possessives

- Use possessive for IBM the company: "IBM's expertise"
- Don't use possessive with product names: not "IBM's Cloud" or "Maximo's"

## Pronouns

- Ensure noun-pronoun agreement
- Make pronoun references clear
- Consider inclusivity

## Quotation Marks

- Use double quotation marks
- Punctuation before closing quotation mark

## Symbols

- Avoid symbols in general text
- Symbols acceptable when space limited (infographics, diagrams)

## Time

- AM/PM in uppercase, no periods
- Ranges: use en dash without spaces, or "from ... to"
